Privacy & Terms

Short version: nothing you put into this tool ever leaves your browser.

Your photos, videos and webcam

Everything Static Grind does to an image, video or webcam feed happens locally, inside your own browser. There is no server behind this tool that your files pass through. Loading a photo, recording your webcam or exporting a result never uploads anything anywhere; it's all handled by your device using URL.createObjectURL, getUserMedia and canvas/WebGL rendering, all of which are local browser APIs. Close the tab and it's gone, there is nothing stored anywhere else to delete.

Camera access

When you click "Use Camera", your browser shows its own permission prompt asking whether to allow access, that's the browser's built-in consent step, not something this site adds on top. If you allow it, the video feed is processed locally the same way an uploaded file is. It is never recorded, transmitted or stored unless you explicitly choose to export a snapshot or recording, which then saves straight to your own device.

Accounts, cookies and tracking

No sign-up, no login, no cookies, no analytics, no tracking scripts of any kind. Nothing here identifies you and nothing is sent to a server.

One thing is stored locally: if you save a preset, the knob positions get written to your browser's localStorage so they're still there when you come back. That's a short list of numbers, it stays on your device, and nobody else can see it, including me. Clearing your browser's site data for staticgrind.com removes it.

Hosting

This site is hosted on Cloudflare. Like any web host, Cloudflare's infrastructure sees standard technical request data as part of serving the page, things like your IP address and request timestamps, the same as any website you visit. That's covered by Cloudflare's own privacy policy, not something this project collects or has access to itself.

Using the tool

Static Grind is provided free, as-is, with no warranty of any kind. Please only process images, video or audio you actually have the rights to use. You're responsible for what you choose to run through it and for how you use anything it produces.
